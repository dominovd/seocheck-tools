YouTube transitioned Shorts monetization from the fixed-fund model to ad revenue sharing on February 1, 2023. The Shorts Fund had paid creators from a fixed monthly pool with capped per-creator amounts. The new model pools Shorts ad revenue and distributes a share to monetized creators based on monthly view contributions.

Eligibility paths changed at the same time. Creators below the long-form Partner Program threshold gained a separate Shorts-only path requiring 1,000 subscribers and 10 million Shorts views in the previous 90 days. The long-form path (1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours over 12 months) continues to apply to long-form monetization.

Music licensing and other Shorts-specific costs are deducted from the Shorts ad revenue pool before distribution. That accounting difference is why Shorts RPM is typically lower than long-form RPM in the same niche, even after the ad-share model rolled out.
