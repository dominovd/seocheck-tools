YouTube announced a new disclosure tool in YouTube Studio for realistic altered or synthetic content. The rule applies when viewers could easily mistake the content for a real person, place, scene, or event, and it does not apply to clearly unrealistic content, animation, special effects, or generative AI used for production help.

The blog listed examples that require disclosure, including face swaps, synthetic voice narration, altered footage of real events or places, and realistic depictions of fictional major events. It also listed examples that do not require disclosure, including script or idea generation, automatic captions, color adjustments, lighting filters, background blur, vintage effects, and beauty filters.

YouTube said most labels will appear in the expanded description, while videos about sensitive topics such as health, news, elections, or finance may also get a more prominent on-screen label. The blog said labels would roll out across YouTube surfaces and formats in the weeks ahead, starting on the mobile app, and said YouTube may add a label itself and may consider future enforcement for repeated non-disclosure.
